## Home Network
- Home networks follow a WLAN setup. They typically have two primary types of ports: Ethernet, and Internet port.

- Many home routers include a radio antenna and a built-in wireless access point. By default, the wireless devices are on the same local network as the devices that are physically plugged into the LAN switch ports. The internet port is the only port that is on a different network in the default configuration.

- An Ethernet port is used to connect a wired device to the internal switch of the home router.

## LAN Wireless Frequencies
- Wireless technologies used in home networks typically are in the unlicensed 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz ranges.
- Bluetooth is a technology that makes use of the 2.4 GHz band. It is limited to low-speed, short-range communications, but has the advantage of communicating with many devices at the same time. 
- Modern wireless LAN techno

Other technologies that use the 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands are the modern wireless LAN technologies that conform to the various IEEE 802.11 standards. Unlike Bluetooth technology, 802.11 devices transmit at a much higher power level giving them a great range and improved throughput.

## Wired Network Technologies
- The most common wired protocol is the Ethernet protocol. It uses a suite of protocols that allow network devices to communicate over a wired LAN connection.
- An Ethernet LAN can connect devices using many different types of wiring.
- Ethernet patch cable, usually unshielded twisted pair is used. It comes in different lengths, with the RJ-45 connectors already installed.

### Category 5e Cable
- Most common wiring used in a LAN.
- Made up of 4 pairs of wires that are twisted to reduce electrical interference.

### Coaxial Cable
- Has an inner wire surrounded by a tubular insulating, that is then surrounded by a tubular conducting shield.
- Also commonly used for cable television (CATV), CCTV security cameras, and radio transmitters.

### Fiber-Optic Cable
- Either glass or plastic; can carry digital information at very high speeds over long distances.
- Very high bandwidth, enabling them to carry large amounts of data.
- The fastest, and most reliable internet connection available.

## Wi-Fi Networks
- Wireless standards for LANs use the 2.4 GHz and 5GHz.

### Wireless Settings
Wi-Fi Alliance is the organization responsible for testing wireless LAN devices. <br><br>

Wireless routers using the 802.11 standards have multiple settings that have to be configured:
- Network mode: Determines the type of technology supported. For example, 802.11b, 802.11g (slower 2.4 GHz), 802.11n (faster 2.4/5 GHz) or Mixed Mode.
    - Mixed mode makes it easier for older devices that do not support the latest standards to connect to the wireless connection.
- Network Name (SSID): Used to identify the WLAN. All devices that wish to participate in the WLAN <b>must have the same SSID.</b>
    - It is the name of the network, allowing other devices to automatically discover the name of the wireless network.
- Standard Channel: Specifies the channel over which communication will occur. Set to auto by default to allow the access point to detemine the optimum channel to use.
- Service Set Identifier (SSID) Broadcast: Determines if the SSID will be broadcast to all devices within range. Set to enabled by default.

### Design Considerations
- SSID: Proper naming, avoid giving away too much information. Don't include the device model or brand name.
- Consider the type of devices that will connect. This requires configuring whether it will have 802.11a, 802,11b, 802.11n, and 802.11ac.
