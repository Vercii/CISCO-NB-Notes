# Module 1
## Network Types

## Internet
- Not owned by an individual or a group.
- Global collection of interconnected networks. Websites and online services operate on top of the internet through the World Wide Web.

### Bit
- Two possible values: 0 or 1.
- Similar to how a light switch works (on/off). 0 and 1 represents False/True states.

## Local Networks
- Ranges from connection between two computers, to networks connecting hundreds or thousands of devices.

### Small Home Networks
- Connects a few computers to each other and to the internet.
- An example of this is a PC setup at home.

### SOHO (small office/home office)
- Networks installed in small offices and/or home offices.
- Lets you share resources between few local users.

### Medium to Large Networks
- Used by corporations and schools.
- Can have multiple locations with hundreds or thousands of interconnected hosts.

### Wide Area Network
- Connects hundreds of millions of computers worldwide.

### Types of Data
- Volunteer Data: Data that you willingly provide, such as social network profiles.
- Inferred Data: Information predicted or derived from other collected data, such as shopping preferences based on purchase history.
- Observed Data: Information collected from your actions. Location tracking, and browsing activity.

Media refers to the physical medium on which the signals are transmitted. Examples of media are copper wire, fiber-optic cable, and electromagnetic waves through the air. 

---

## Methods of Data Transmission
- Three Common Methods of Signal Transmission:
    - Electrical signals - Transmission is achieved by representing data as electrical pulses on copper wire.
    - Optical signals - Transmission is achieved by converting the electrical signals into light pulses.
    - Wireless signals - Transmission is achieved by using infrared, microwave, or radio waves through the air.

## Bandwidth
- The capacity of a medium to carry data.
- Digital bandwidth measures the amount of data that can flow from one place to another.
- Measured in the number of bits that (theoretically) can be sent across the media in a second.
    - Kbps: thousands of bits per second
    - Mbps: millions of bits per second
    - Gbps: billions of bits per second

## Throughput
- The measure of transfer of bits across the media.
- Does not usually match the specified bandwidth.
- Cannot be faster than the slowest link of path from sending device to receiving device.

### Factors influencing throughput
- The amount of data being sent and received over the connection.
- The types of data being transmitted.
- The latency created by the number of network devices encountered between source and destination. 

## Key Takeaways
- Internet is a collection of interconnected networks.
- Bandwidth is <b>theoretical</b> capacity, throughput is the <b>actual</b> transfer rate.
- Data can travel through electrical, optical, or wireless signals.
- Network size ranges from home setups to global WANs.
